👨‍🎓 About Me

Hi, I am Jinpeng Lu, a 2025-entry master's student in Information and Communication Engineering at the University of Science and Technology of China. I work with Prof. Zhiwei Xiong. Previously, I received my B.S. in Data Science and Big Data Technology (Mathematics discipline) from Harbin Institute of Technology, Shenzhen.

My current research asks whether video generation models maintain coherent world states as the camera moves, events occur outside the field of view, and previously observed regions return. I also study embodied intelligence and vision-language-action (VLA) models, focusing on how internal representations shape decisions and actions.

I design diagnostic benchmarks and analyze model representations and behavior. My first-author work WRBench studies world-state persistence under viewpoint changes. I also co-authored VLA-Trace and Pelican-Unify 1.0. Earlier work in medical AI provides my foundation in efficient multimodal 3D perception.

I also have an exploratory interest in how claims about recursive self-improvement (RSI) could be tested, verified, and evaluated for safety.

Current World Models Lack a Persistent State Core

J. Lu, D. Zhu, H. Shi, L. Cai, G. Tang, Y. Chen, J. Cao, D. Tang, Y. Zhang, Y. Dai, X. Ju.

  • Question: Do video generation models preserve an evolving world state when the camera changes what is visible?
  • Approach: WRBench uses controlled camera motion and human-calibrated tests to evaluate visual integrity and state consistency after re-observation.
  • Finding: Across 9,600 videos from 23 models, visual quality and successful re-observation did not reliably imply correct event-state consistency.

🔬 Research

Research questions spanning video world models, embodied AI, and the evaluation of self-improving systems.

🌍 Video World Models & Generative Video Evaluation

Testing whether generated worlds remain coherent when the viewpoint changes.

I study whether video generation models preserve evolving world states when subjects leave the field of view and later reappear. WRBench treats camera motion as a controlled change in what the model can observe, then tests whether events remain correctly represented after re-observation.

The benchmark separates camera control and visual integrity from the harder question of state persistence. This research combines human-calibrated evaluation, open benchmark resources, and failure analysis across model families and camera-control settings.

🤖 Embodied AI & VLA Analysis

Tracing how multimodal representations become decisions and actions.

My collaborative work includes VLA-Trace, which follows VLA models from representation dynamics to causal control pathways and rollout behavior. Pelican-Unify 1.0 studies a shared model for visual understanding, reasoning, future video generation, and action prediction.

These projects motivate my interest in making embodied models more interpretable: where task-relevant information appears, how it influences action, and where that connection breaks down.

🔁 Recursive Self-Improvement (Exploratory Interest)

Testing capability-improvement claims with verifiable and safety-aware evidence.

I am exploring how claims about recursive self-improvement could be tested, verified, and evaluated for safety. This is an emerging research interest rather than a completed research program.

🏥 Earlier Foundation: Medical Vision

Efficient 3D perception, multimodal segmentation, and transfer limits.

My earlier work develops efficient and robust segmentation methods for PET/CT and 3D medical images. In VeloxSeg, I combine JL-guided convolution, paired window attention, and spatially decoupled knowledge transfer for lightweight multimodal 3D segmentation.

I also study transfer limits of visual foundation models in medicine, including DINOv3-style self-supervised features across 2D/3D classification, segmentation, and registration tasks.
