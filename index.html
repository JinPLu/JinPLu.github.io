👨‍🎓 About Me

Hi, I am Jinpeng Lu. I am a master's student in Information and Communication Engineering at the University of Science and Technology of China, where I work with Prof. Zhiwei Xiong. Before coming to USTC, I studied Data Science and Big Data Technology at Harbin Institute of Technology, Shenzhen.

A question I keep coming back to is simple: if a video model looks away and then looks back, does the world still make sense? I use this question to study video world models and generative video evaluation. I am also interested in embodied AI, especially how a model's internal representations influence the actions it takes.

My recent work includes WRBench, a benchmark for testing whether video models preserve events as the camera moves. I also co-authored VLA-Trace on how representations lead to behavior and Pelican-Unify 1.0 on joint future-video and action generation. Earlier, I worked on efficient multimodal 3D medical segmentation.

Outside my main projects, I am exploring how claims of recursive self-improvement could be tested and evaluated safely. If you work on related questions, I would be happy to exchange ideas. Feel free to email me.

Current World Models Lack a Persistent State Core

J. Lu, D. Zhu, H. Shi, L. Cai, G. Tang, Y. Chen, J. Cao, D. Tang, Y. Zhang, Y. Dai, X. Ju.

  • Question: When the camera changes only what is observed, does one task-relevant event state continue to evolve coherently?
  • Approach: WRBench separates camera execution, visual integrity, visible evolution, re-observation support, and conditional endpoint consistency across four native camera-control interfaces.
  • Finding: Across 9,600 videos from 23 models, better view synthesis and re-observation support did not reliably yield correct event endpoints.

🔬 Research

Research questions spanning video world models, embodied AI, and the evaluation of self-improving systems.

🌍 Video World Models & Generative Video Evaluation

Testing whether one event remains coherent when only the observation changes.

I study whether video generation models maintain a single evolving, task-relevant world state when a target is visible, temporarily unobservable, and later reobserved. WRBench treats camera motion as an observational intervention: it should change what is seen, not rewrite the event itself.

The benchmark separates camera execution, visual integrity, visible spatial/state evolution, re-observation support, and conditional endpoint consistency. It uses open-weight evaluators calibrated with 4,302 human judgments over 1,741 video pairs to diagnose failure modes across model families and camera-control settings.

🤖 Embodied AI & VLA Analysis

Tracing how multimodal representations become decisions and actions.

My collaborative work includes VLA-Trace, which follows VLA models from representation dynamics to causal control pathways and rollout behavior. Pelican-Unify 1.0 studies a shared model for visual understanding, reasoning, future video generation, and action prediction.

These projects motivate my interest in making embodied models more interpretable: where task-relevant information appears, how it influences action, and where that connection breaks down.

🔁 Recursive Self-Improvement (Exploratory Interest)

Testing capability-improvement claims with verifiable and safety-aware evidence.

I am exploring how claims about recursive self-improvement could be tested, verified, and evaluated for safety. This is an emerging research interest rather than a completed research program.

🏥 Earlier Foundation: Medical Vision

Efficient 3D perception, multimodal segmentation, and transfer limits.

My earlier work develops efficient and robust segmentation methods for PET/CT and 3D medical images. In VeloxSeg, I combine JL-guided convolution, paired window attention, and spatially decoupled knowledge transfer for lightweight multimodal 3D segmentation.

I also study transfer limits of visual foundation models in medicine, including DINOv3-style self-supervised features across 2D/3D classification, segmentation, and registration tasks.
